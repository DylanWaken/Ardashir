Count at the right level. The additions use three instanced components, two door-assembly mesh components, and one skeletal mesh component. The three instanced components contain thirty placements in total: three pots, three stems, and twenty-four leaves. That is six added primitive-bearing components, not thirty-six actors and not a measured draw count. Sections, passes, culling, and renderer-specific paths determine the work that eventually reaches the GPU. The earlier twelve-tile accounting exercise was a deliberately controlled submission plan; it is not an estimate of this captured frame.

Move the hinge; keep the door mesh

The hinge is at (−150,200,0) cm. A 100 cm wide, 200 cm high door starts along +X and rotates through +90° yaw toward +Y. Its six-centimeter thickness clears the baseline blocks. The indicator starts at hinge-relative (60,0,150), so its center travels from (−90,200,150) to (−150,260,150). Compare these values with the earlier attachment diagram: the computed example and the captured hierarchy now describe the same object.

At the scene boundary, this edit is a transform change with consequences for bounds, current/previous placement, visibility, and supported secondary representations. It is not a request to rewrite the door's indices. The emissive indicator may contribute through supported lighting paths, but its presence creates no additional point or rect light component. Adding such a component would be a separate scene-service contribution, as the light chapter explains.

Change a pose inside one placed object

The mannequin stands at (65,110,0) cm, in front of the matte block. The two images below evaluate ThirdPersonWalk at 0.10 s and 0.45 s, keeping the actor at the same location and −90° yaw. We can therefore isolate a deformation update from a rigid placement update. The template mesh and animation are actual Unreal assets; the two-bone sleeve strip earlier in the essay is a small arithmetic model of the same kind of operation, not a claim about this asset's particular weights.

A real-time implementation must retain a coherent previous pose and placement when it emits motion. It must also supply bounds that enclose the deformed surface and negotiate which shadow and ray paths can consume that geometry. Merely seeing the mannequin in a lit image does not establish that software distance fields or a particular GI cache represent its moving triangles. The baseline distance-field and card captures later in the essay remain explicitly about the seven rigid cube components.
