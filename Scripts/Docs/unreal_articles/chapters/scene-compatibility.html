Extend the room until its geometry contract is explicit

We have followed seven cube components through the renderer. Now keep the same four-by-five-by-three-meter room, the same red and green walls, and the same matte and glossy blocks, and ask what changes when we furnish it. Three pots of repeated leaves introduce instancing. A mannequin introduces a skeleton. A cable, a breakable panel, smoke, and a basin each expose a different contract. The room stays our reference frame: every new object must explain what data it adds, who updates that data, and which rendering consumers can use it. This is the proposed Arda compatibility contract, not a claim that Arda already implements these adapters.

Separate the room's states before reading the diagrams. The baseline capture contains exactly five enclosing static-mesh surfaces and two static-mesh blocks, plus a separate rectangular light. The captured extension adds the mannequin, three pot instances, three stem instances, twenty-four leaf instances, and the hinged door with its indicator; the original seven placements stay fixed. The specialty fixtures below are explicit schematic alternatives or additions. A diagram of a cloth strip, groom, Landscape exhibit, Water body, or volume does not claim that the associated plugin is running in those captures. Nor do the alternatives all coexist in one crowded room.

1. Conventional static meshes

Start with the matte block we already measured. Its world-space size comes from its component transform; its triangles still come from the shared cube asset. The door in the captured extension uses the same rigid-surface contract even while its parent hinge moves. Translation or rotation alone does not turn either object into a skeletal mesh.

Core adapter target · UStaticMeshComponent. The asset supplies indexed triangle sections, vertex attributes, material slots, bounds, and conventional LOD resources; the component supplies placement and rendering flags. Arda needs an adapter that preserves section/material identity, UV and tangent conventions, current and previous transforms, LOD selection, and culling bounds. It must then provide the appropriate depth, base, shadow, and velocity work. Ray-tracing geometry and distance-field or card representations are additional products, not fields that magically appear because the raster mesh loaded. Material blending and World Position Offset can change which passes are needed. A rigid object moving every frame still belongs to this geometry family; “static mesh” describes its asset representation, not an immovable actor. StaticMeshComponent.h

2. Nanite-enabled triangle geometry

For a specialty variant, mount a finely sculpted panel on the back wall and enable Nanite for that asset. Keep its placement and material role fixed while changing the geometry backend. The baseline cube room does not by itself demonstrate cluster streaming or prove that an Arda conventional-mesh importer can consume Nanite resources.

Feature mode · typically UStaticMeshComponent, also the supported instanced, spline, skinned, and collection paths. Supporting a conventional static mesh does not mean Arda can consume Nanite's cluster hierarchy, streaming pages, visibility results, or programmable raster/shading behavior. Arda must either implement a compatible virtualized-geometry representation or explicitly select an available conventional fallback with a stated fidelity difference. Displacement, masking, deformation, shadow rendering, and ray tracing each need their own capability decision. An acceleration structure may use a fallback or another supported Nanite representation; it is not automatically the same triangles selected for the camera. The inspected code has a separate skinned-Nanite gate, so the common claim that Nanite categorically means rigid static geometry is already too narrow for this revision. SkinnedMeshComponent.cpp: Nanite feature gate See also Epic's versioned Nanite overview.

3. ISM, HISM, foliage, and landscape grass

Use the three pots, three stems, and twenty-four leaf placements in the captured room extension as the smallest visible instancing test. These are thirty instances across three ISM components, with one shared mesh per component. Moving one leaf changes its instance data; it need not duplicate the leaf mesh. Those geometric leaves use ISM and a masked, two-sided-foliage material; the material shading model does not turn their source into a FoliageActor. A proposed foliage-authored version of the same planter, or grass generated on the floor exhibit, tests additional producer contracts that the ISM capture alone cannot establish.

Core adapter target, with feature modes · UInstancedStaticMeshComponent, UHierarchicalInstancedStaticMeshComponent, UFoliageInstancedStaticMeshComponent, and UGrassInstancedStaticMeshComponent. The raw surface is shared; instance transforms, previous transforms, custom data, visibility, and identifiers vary. HISM adds hierarchical organization, while foliage and grass systems own placement and regeneration policies. Arda needs stable instance identity, bulk updates, per-instance data, culling/LOD behavior, and material access to instance variation. Expanding all instances into unrelated duplicated meshes can reproduce a picture but loses the memory, update, and GPU-driven behavior needed at foliage scale. A supported Nanite mesh changes the geometry backend, not the ownership of instance data. Shadow and ray representations must preserve the relevant instances, and masked or displaced vegetation needs consistent bounds and coverage. InstancedStaticMeshComponent.h HierarchicalInstancedStaticMeshComponent.h FoliageInstancedStaticMeshComponent.h GrassInstancedStaticMeshComponent.h

4. Skinned meshes, morphs, cloth, and mesh deformers

The room mannequin supplies the counterexample to moving a whole rigid object. During its walk pose, vertices on the forearm move relative to the torso, so one actor transform is insufficient. The two captured mannequin poses demonstrate a real skeletal component. A facial morph, simulated garment, manually controlled pose, or repeated skinned instances of that mannequin is a separate feature test, not something established by one successful skeletal capture.

Core skinning target plus feature modes · USkeletalMeshComponent through USkinnedMeshComponent. Inputs include indexed geometry, bone indices and weights, a pose, optional morph deltas, cloth output, and mesh-deformer results. Arda's adapter must define the deformation order and provide current and previous deformed positions, shading attributes, material sections, and updated bounds. A bind-pose mesh plus an actor transform is insufficient. Velocity and shadow passes need compatible deformation; ray-tracing geometry needs an appropriate build or update, and GI representations can have different fidelity or update cadence. Nanite skinning is separately gated in this checkout and does not prove that every morph, cloth, or deformer combination follows that path. Record the specific supported combination and fallback rather than advertising one Boolean “skeletal support.” SkeletalMeshComponent.h SkinnedMeshComponent.cpp: skinned rendering modes

The adapter should target the skinned/deformation contract rather than recognize only USkeletalMeshComponent. UPoseableMeshComponent supplies manually controlled poses. UInstancedSkinnedMeshComponent adds instance transforms, animation indices, custom data, and previous transforms, so instancing is not limited to static meshes. In the Chaos Cloth Asset plugin, UChaosClothComponent also derives from USkinnedMeshComponent and accepts cloth or outfit assets. Each producer needs its appropriate pose/deformation handoff; none is correctly covered by checking only the skeletal-component class name. PoseableMeshComponent.h InstancedSkinnedMeshComponent.h Chaos ClothAsset/ClothComponent.h

5. Landscape heightfields and Nanite Landscape

Place a small raised terrain exhibit on the room floor, leaving the original floor cube intact. In one variant, author the exhibit as Landscape height and layer tiles; in another, feed a Runtime Virtual Texture height producer to Virtual Heightfield Mesh. This deliberately unusual indoor fixture lets us compare terrain providers without introducing a second outdoor scene. Its dimensions must be chosen through the provider's valid grid and scale settings, not by assuming a Landscape is an arbitrary mesh patch.

Terrain adapter plus feature mode · ULandscapeComponent and ULandscapeNaniteComponent. Conventional Landscape uses tiled height and weight data, terrain topology/LOD rules, material layers, and hole information. Arda must preserve tile adjacency, coordinate/scale conventions, layer sampling, holes, bounds, and a compatible terrain surface. Nanite Landscape adds derived Nanite mesh data through a distinct component type. It does not make every heightfield use disappear: collision, water-related use, and other systems can still depend on the original terrain representation. Shadows and GI must consume a representation with the same shape and holes; a generic flat grid with a color texture is not terrain compatibility. The two component classes and Epic's Nanite Landscape documentation make the coexistence explicit. LandscapeComponent.h LandscapeNaniteComponent.h

A separate plugin provider, UVirtualHeightfieldMeshComponent, renders a heightfield from a Runtime Virtual Texture heightmap. It is not a renamed ULandscapeComponent. Arda needs the virtual-texture producer and sampling contract, min/max height data, tile/LOD selection, material binding, and the provider's explicit velocity/shadow capabilities. The separate RVT producer in the diagram illustrates this alternative data source. A static export of the resulting surface is a possible approximation, but it loses live updates to the virtual height field. VirtualHeightfieldMeshComponent.h

6. Spline-deformed meshes and spline data

Run a cable along the green-wall skirting and bend it around the glossy block. The centerline stores control information; a spline-mesh component supplies a source surface and deforms it along that curve. Change the cable bend and inspect its shadow and previous-frame positions as well as its main-view silhouette.

Feature mode · USplineMeshComponent; distinguish USplineComponent. A spline mesh deforms a source static mesh using spline parameters, roll, scale, and an axis convention. Arda must evaluate compatible positions and tangents, expand conservative bounds, and retain prior deformation for motion when needed. A spline component stores curve information and can draw a debug visualization, but it does not inherently generate a production cable surface. It actually derives from UPrimitiveComponent in this source; inheritance alone therefore cannot be used to infer a production geometry contract. Its debug draw relevance is gated by spline visualization settings. Conventional or Nanite spline rendering, shadows, ray geometry, and Lumen capture each require consistent deformation or an explicit approximation. SplineMeshComponent.h SplineComponent.h SplineComponent.cpp: debug draw relevance

7. Procedural mesh sections

Build the back-wall test panel by submitting its vertex arrays and triangle indices from code. Add one triangle or change a corner position while leaving the rest of the room unchanged. This isolates the adapter's section creation, buffer lifetime, bounds update, and material assignment from any particular mesh-generation algorithm.

Plugin scope · UProceduralMeshComponent. Its section-creation API accepts vertex positions, triangle indices, normals, UV sets, colors, and tangents. Arda needs a section-based mutable mesh adapter with validated indices, material assignment, explicit update lifetimes, and bounds recomputation. Collision creation is a separate request in the API; a collision mesh is not a substitute for the rendering buffers. Position-only updates and topology replacements should invalidate different resources where possible. Conventional raster, depth, shadow, velocity, and optional ray geometry need compatible updates; prebuilt static-mesh distance fields or Nanite pages should not be assumed to exist for arbitrary newly generated triangles. This family is about application-provided geometry, not any particular algorithm used to generate it. ProceduralMeshComponent.h: section data contract

8. Editable dynamic meshes

Now use an editable mesh object for that same wall test panel and cut a notch into its lower edge. The new boundary changes topology and potentially normal/UV seams; this is richer than updating four positions in a fixed procedural section. The visual checkpoint is the notch in both the image and the shadow, with a conservative updated bound.

Feature mode · UDynamicMeshComponent through UBaseDynamicMeshComponent. The source representation is FDynamicMesh3 through a mesh object, with attributes and potentially changing topology. Arda must convert or directly maintain compatible render sections, attribute seams, material IDs, and update regions. Unreal exposes different notifications for full mesh changes and more limited attribute or position changes, demonstrating why a single “upload mesh again” operation misses useful update semantics. A runtime cut can invalidate index buffers, bounds, acceleration structures, and secondary surface representations together. Raster support alone does not establish automatic Nanite, distance-field, or GI-cache support for every edit; each representation needs an explicit rebuild/update policy. DynamicMeshComponent.h: mesh representation DynamicMeshComponent.h: update notifications

9. Geometry-cache animation

Hang a short cloth strip from the wall test panel and bake its motion to geometry samples. We can then ask for the sample at time t without solving cloth simulation in the renderer or evaluating a skeleton. This differs from the optional live cloth on the mannequin: the source of the positions is the cache timeline.

Plugin scope · UGeometryCacheComponent. Geometry caches provide time-sampled mesh tracks, transforms, bounds, and applicable motion information; topology can differ according to the cache content and codec. Arda's adapter needs track/time selection, interpolation rules where supported, sample streaming, materials, and previous-position or motion-vector semantics. Treating every sample as a skeletal pose invents a bone model the asset does not contain. Conversely, displaying only one frame loses the essential behavior. Shadows and ray-tracing geometry must update with the chosen sample, and expensive rebuilds or missing secondary GI representations must be reported as capability limits. Alembic is a possible content route into such a cache, not the runtime component itself. GeometryCacheComponent.h: track-oriented component GeometryCacheComponent.h: motion-vector controls

10. Chaos Geometry Collections

Replace the wall test panel with a pre-fractured collection. The intact view should resemble the earlier panel; after impact, the visible pieces have independent transforms and newly exposed interior faces. Keeping this one object and camera fixed makes it obvious why one static combined mesh cannot describe both states.

Feature mode · UGeometryCollectionComponent. A collection carries mesh pieces, a transform hierarchy, material assignments, and simulation-driven visibility or fracture state. Arda needs piece identity and transforms, the relationship between parent clusters and visible pieces, interior-face materials, bounds, and current/previous state. A single combined triangle mesh may reproduce the intact object but cannot reproduce the transition to independently moving fragments without additional structure. Conventional and Nanite collection rendering are separate supported modes to negotiate. Shadow and ray representations must follow active pieces; secondary lighting representations may update at a different rate. Chaos simulation itself can remain a distinct producer of state if the compatibility goal is rendering its output, but the rendered result must preserve the simulation's piece activation and motion. GeometryCollectionComponent.h

11. BSP/model surfaces and brush visualization

Consider an alternative authoring history for the back wall: construct it with brushes and cut a doorway. The baseline room uses cube components, so this is not a reinterpretation of its measured asset inventory. It is a compatibility test that asks whether a cooked model surface with the same wall role can enter Arda without the editor's brush workflow.

Feature mode · UModelComponent; distinguish UBrushComponent. Older or blockout-oriented levels can contain model surfaces derived from BSP/brush operations. Arda needs the resulting polygon/triangle surfaces, surface material assignments, texture-coordinate mapping, bounds, and visibility semantics if these levels are in scope. It need not execute the editor's entire constructive modeling workflow merely to render a cooked result. A brush component can also serve editor or volume visualization, so copying its displayed wireframe is not necessarily the intended production surface. Conventional raster compatibility should be assessed separately from ray tracing, Nanite conversion, and GI data; converting a model to a supported static mesh is an explicit migration path with its own fidelity checks. ModelComponent.h BrushComponent.h

12. Niagara and Cascade sprite particles

Put a small effects station in the room's open-front area and emit smoke motes above it. In the sprite version, each particle record becomes a facing quad. Moving the camera exposes the orientation rule; overlapping motes expose sorting and compositing. Neither test requires pretending the smoke consists of independent UObject mesh components.

Plugin scope · UNiagaraComponent with UNiagaraSpriteRendererProperties. Input records carry positions, sizes, rotations, colors, velocities, and material attributes produced by CPU or GPU simulation. Arda needs compatible particle-buffer bindings, billboard orientation, sorting and bounds, indirect counts where applicable, and the renderer's material-input convention. A particle is not a separate UObject mesh component. Translucent sprites need their appropriate compositing and lighting path; masked sprites have different coverage/shadow behavior. Motion, shadow casting, ray visibility, and participation in GI are renderer/material features to negotiate, not consequences of drawing a quad. Matching the simulation output format and sprite expansion is the rendering contract even if the simulation implementation lives elsewhere. NiagaraComponent.h NiagaraSpriteRendererProperties.h

Legacy Cascade content uses UParticleSystemComponent and its emitter/module data instead of Niagara. Its sprite output has a similar low-level quad form but different simulation, attribute, and sorting conventions, so it requires a separate source adapter. The same distinction applies to its mesh, beam, and ribbon modes described below. Supporting Niagara does not automatically import Cascade assets. ParticleSystemComponent.h ParticleModuleRequired.h

13. Niagara and Cascade ribbons and beams

At the same effects station, replace the smoke motes with a continuous light trail. Ordered points now define a connected strip, so adjacency, width, and UV flow matter. A ribbon and a beam-like effect can share this generated-surface mechanism while receiving different point arrangements.

Plugin scope · UNiagaraComponent with UNiagaraRibbonRendererProperties. A ribbon uses ordered points, ribbon identifiers, width, tangent/facing data, tessellation, and UV-distribution rules to construct connected surface geometry. Beam templates can use this machinery with an appropriate point-generation arrangement; do not invent a required UNiagaraBeamRendererProperties class. Arda needs ordering, connectivity, width expansion, interpolation, materials, bounds, and previous-frame behavior. Independent point sprites cannot reproduce a continuous ribbon's silhouette or texture flow. Sorting, translucency, shadow behavior, and ray/GI representation remain separate choices, particularly when a rapidly changing trail's topology or length changes every frame. NiagaraRibbonRendererProperties.h

For legacy Cascade, the exact source modes include UParticleModuleTypeDataRibbon and UParticleModuleTypeDataBeam2 within UParticleSystemComponent. Their tessellation, connectivity, and material-coordinate conventions must be translated explicitly. Unlike the Niagara example above, a named Beam2 type-data class does exist here. The shared graphical forms explain what can be reused in Arda's renderer; they do not erase the producer-specific contract. ParticleModuleTypeDataRibbon.h ParticleModuleTypeDataBeam2.h

14. Niagara and Cascade mesh particles

Use the station once more, this time emitting small fragments resembling chips from the wall test panel. The fragments have visible thickness and orientation, so mesh resources accompany their particle state. This reuses mesh rendering machinery but has simulation-owned counts and lifetimes rather than the persistent instance list used by the planter.

Plugin scope · UNiagaraComponent with UNiagaraMeshRendererProperties. Mesh particles combine particle attributes with selected mesh resources, material overrides, and renderer-specific scaling/orientation. Arda can reuse mesh and instance machinery, but it must adapt particle-driven counts, selection, bounds, previous transforms, and simulation-buffer ownership. The instanced data source and lifetime differ from a persistent ISM component. Conventional, Nanite-related plugin paths, shadows, and ray visibility must be identified separately for the selected renderer and platform. A generic “Niagara supported” label that handles only sprites would silently lose debris meshes; a mesh-only implementation would lose smoke and trails. These three particle entries are distinct renderer modes under the same high-level component. NiagaraMeshRendererProperties.h

Cascade's UParticleModuleTypeDataMesh is the legacy mesh-particle producer and likewise needs its own attribute adapter. Niagara also has renderer families beyond the three surface forms illustrated here: UNiagaraVolumeRendererProperties connects to heterogeneous volume rendering, while UNiagaraLightRendererProperties and UNiagaraDecalRendererProperties produce scene-service contributions. Thus the three particle surface entries are not an exhaustive inventory of Niagara renderer outputs. ParticleModuleTypeDataMesh.h NiagaraLightRendererProperties.h NiagaraDecalRendererProperties.h

15. Groom strands, cards, and hair meshes

Give the room mannequin hair as a separate specialty variant. At close range, draw strands; at a chosen LOD, substitute cards or a coarse hair mesh. Root binding must follow the mannequin's deformation in every supported representation. The captured mannequin poses alone do not demonstrate a Groom asset, strand shading, or hair ray-tracing support.

Plugin scope · UGroomComponent. Strands use curve samples with hair-specific attributes and specialized visibility/shading; cards and meshes use surface geometry approximating groups of hairs. Arda must preserve the selected representation, root binding, deformation/simulation output, LOD transitions, material requirements, and bounds. Treating strand data as an ordinary indexed triangle buffer omits the curve-to-coverage problem and hair scattering. Cards require alpha/coverage and orientation behavior consistent with their textures. Shadows and ray tracing have representation-specific resources, and GI behavior is not established merely by a successful main-view image. The component explicitly carries strand, card, and mesh material/resource concepts; compatibility must state which modes are supported. GroomComponent.h: representation-specific materials

16. LiDAR point clouds

Imagine surveying this exact room, retaining measured positions and colors rather than rebuilding its walls as triangle meshes. The illustration uses synthetic samples of the room planes to show the data shape; it is not a real LiDAR capture. Changing the point footprint exposes gaps and overlap, which a watertight triangle importer would not encounter.

Plugin scope · ULidarPointCloudComponent. Raw points carry positions and appearance data, with spatial organization and point-rendering controls. Arda needs point selection/streaming, size or footprint interpretation, color mapping, camera behavior, and bounds. Rendering a small billboard or splat for each selected point is different from assuming the source contains a watertight triangle mesh. A reconstructed triangle proxy, if generated for another purpose, is a separate representation. Shadow casting, ray intersections, and GI coverage must be implemented or approximated deliberately; gaps between samples and point-size choices change visible coverage. The component exposes point size, shape, orientation, and coloration settings that belong in the adapter contract. LidarPointCloudComponent.h: point appearance controls

17. Sparse heterogeneous volumes: smoke, fire, and fog

Replace the station's smoke sprites with an actual density field. Looking through the same bounded region now accumulates scattering, absorption, and emission along a ray. The cube around the field limits the work; it is not the smoke's opaque skin. This is the clean comparison between a surface impostor and a participating medium in one room.

Feature mode · UHeterogeneousVolumeComponent, with sparse-volume assets/materials as applicable. Inputs describe spatial fields such as density, scattering, absorption, and emission, potentially animated through Sparse Volume Texture frames. Arda needs volume bounds and transforms, field sampling/streaming, time selection, step/integration policy, volume-domain material evaluation, and appropriate light/shadow interaction. A cube can bound the work, but shading only the cube's faces cannot reproduce the smoke. Ordinary surface GBuffer, triangle BLAS, and card-capture support do not imply volume support. The component's resolution, frame transform, and animation fields expose this different data model; Epic's volume documentation details the renderer-specific path. HeterogeneousVolumeComponent.h

Niagara can also produce this renderer family through UNiagaraVolumeRendererProperties, whose UseHeterogeneousVolumes() returns true. In the inspected header its source mode is Emitter, and particle source mode is explicitly left as future work; this is not evidence that arbitrary per-particle volumes are supported. Arda needs that emitter-level volume's material bindings, transform, size/resolution, and sampling parameters in addition to the volume consumer itself. NiagaraVolumeRendererProperties.h: volume path and source-mode limit

18. Volumetric cloud layers

For this service test only, cut an optional skylight into the room's ceiling and look upward from the same interior. Clouds belong to the external atmosphere visible through that opening. Scaling a planetary cloud layer down into the room would change the model being tested; indoor localized smoke belongs to the preceding volume family. The baseline room has a closed ceiling and no such cloud contribution.

Feature mode · UVolumetricCloudComponent, which derives from USceneComponent. Cloud layer altitude/height, a volume material, tracing limits, and lighting/shadow settings describe the rendered medium. Arda needs a cloud/atmosphere adapter with compatible density coordinates, ray integration, aerial perspective, temporal treatment, and light interaction. This cannot be discovered solely by enumerating UPrimitiveComponent objects. A sky-textured sphere is an explicitly different approximation. Main-view, reflection, and shadow ray sampling have separate controls in the component, so a single generic triangle-pass capability flag cannot describe cloud compatibility. VolumetricCloudComponent.h: layer and sampling settings

19. Paper2D sprites, flipbooks, and tile maps

Mount a small control display on the green wall. One implementation uses a Paper2D status sprite, an animated flipbook icon, and tile-map cells for a small layout diagram. All remain planar, yet the importer must preserve three different sources of UVs, frame selection, and ordering.

Plugin scope · UPaperSpriteComponent, UPaperFlipbookComponent, and UPaperTileMapComponent. Sprites provide planar render geometry and texture regions; flipbooks choose sprite frames over time; tile maps arrange cells into layers with per-tile appearance and transforms. Arda needs pivots, pixel-to-world scale, UV/atlas handling, frame timing, layer ordering, material/blend behavior, and bounds. Flattening a tile map into a photograph loses parallax and editable layer semantics; treating a flipbook as one permanent quad texture loses its animation contract. Depth, shadow, and lighting behavior depend on the chosen materials and renderer settings. These surfaces may share low-level triangle code while still requiring different source adapters. 20. Billboards and material billboards

Attach floating markers to the matte block and glossy block. As the room camera moves, their surfaces turn toward the viewer. These are deliberate in-game labels; editor light icons or component gizmos should not be exported merely because they also use billboards.

Feature mode · UBillboardComponent and UMaterialBillboardComponent. A billboard describes a facing quad or similar generated surface, its texture/material, sizing, and potentially distance-dependent appearance. Arda must reproduce camera-facing orientation, screen/world size interpretation, bounds, and depth/translucency behavior. Do not replace all billboards with fixed world planes: their silhouette changes differently as the camera moves. Some uses are editor icons and should be excluded from a production-scene export according to visibility flags; others are intentional in-game visuals. Shadow, ray, and GI inclusion must follow the content's role and explicit capability, rather than manufacturing solid geometry from every icon. BillboardComponent.h MaterialBillboardComponent.h

21. World-space text

Place the label ROOM 01 next to the control display. Changing it to ROOM 100 can change glyph count, layout, geometry, and bounds while leaving the component transform fixed. A font texture plus a transform is therefore not enough to reproduce the component.

Feature mode · UTextRenderComponent. Text content, font metrics/atlas data, alignment, world scale, color, and material settings determine the generated glyph surfaces. Arda needs compatible text layout and glyph sampling as well as a rendering adapter; loading only the component transform and font texture is insufficient. Text changes can change geometry and bounds. Depth, masking/translucency, and material lighting determine its pass behavior. Ray and GI treatment should be explicitly scoped; a distant label does not automatically need a solid volumetric proxy. This is world-space scene text, distinct from a screen-space UI overlay or arbitrary rich UMG widget composition. TextRenderComponent.h

22. World-space widget surfaces

Keep the control display's location but implement it as a UMG widget rendered to a target. Compare a flat panel with the component's cylindrical mode. The UI producer supplies the image and its redraw timing; the world-space surface supplies geometry, depth, and transform behavior.

Feature mode · UWidgetComponent. In world space, the widget is rendered to a target and displayed using a configured geometric surface; the component exposes plane/cylinder geometry modes, draw size, and render-target access. Arda needs either compatible widget evaluation or an explicitly supplied rendered UI surface, then the correct transform, curvature, material, alpha handling, timing, and bounds. Screen-space mode belongs to a different composition path and must not be exported as an arbitrary world quad. Interaction/hit testing is a separate compatibility concern beyond drawing. Shadows and GI depend on the surface/material policy; preserving readable UI usually requires deliberate treatment rather than assuming ordinary lit opaque shading. WidgetComponent.h: spaces and geometry modes WidgetComponent.h: render target and sizing

23. Water bodies and their generated surfaces

Place a shallow basin near the glossy block. Its shell can remain an ordinary static mesh; the optional Water-plugin fixture supplies the water-body description and an appropriate generated or custom surface. Do not infer Water-body compatibility from a blue plane or from a material using a water shading model. The basin is a controlled small test, not a claim that all river/ocean features have been exercised.

Plugin scope · UWaterBodyComponent and the Water plugin's mesh-producing components. Water bodies carry shape, material, and water-specific state and can contribute to the shared water mesh or use alternative primitive/static-mesh components. Arda needs the resulting surface topology/LOD, wave displacement, bounds, materials, depth/underwater semantics, and relevant scene textures. Importing only the basin boundary or a water-body spline does not reproduce its rendered water. Reflection, refraction, shadowing, and GI behavior are specialized material/rendering features to negotiate; a blue opaque mesh is merely an explicit fallback. The source exposes both whether a body affects the water mesh and a list of alternative primitive components, making the description/geometry distinction visible. 24. Scene contributors that are not ordinary geometry

Return to the actual ceiling RectLight: it illuminates all suitable room surfaces without contributing another cube mesh. Then propose a decal on the matte block, a bounded fog patch near the effects station, or a changed view color grade. Their different visual effects explain why a compatible scene importer needs more than a primitive-component walk.

Separate scene services · UDecalComponent, ULightComponentBase and derived lights, USkyAtmosphereComponent, and UPostProcessComponent. These derive from USceneComponent in the inspected hierarchy rather than from the ordinary mesh/primitive path. Decals project material effects onto eligible receivers; lights supply illumination; atmosphere supplies participating-medium behavior; post-process components supply view settings. Arda needs corresponding service adapters, receiver/pass rules, and blending/priority semantics. Their gizmos or volume bounds are not the final visual effect. Separately, UShapeComponent descendants such as collision boxes, spheres, and capsules, brush volumes, and spline debug lines can derive from primitive classes while serving collision or visualization. Include them as visible production geometry only when content actually requests that visualization. No automatic shadow/ray/GI mesh should be invented from every editor outline. Fog deserves its own service adapter as well. UExponentialHeightFogComponent describes an exponential height distribution and can enable a distinct volumetric-fog mode; ULocalFogVolumeComponent supplies bounded radial/height extinction and scattering. Both derive from USceneComponent. Neither is equivalent to importing a Sparse Volume Texture into UHeterogeneousVolumeComponent. Arda must preserve density distributions, transforms, lighting/composition rules, and feature settings; a triangle shadow or a Lumen card cannot stand in for participating-medium integration. Niagara light and decal renderer outputs must feed the corresponding services while respecting their simulation/source-mode gates. The room experiments now suggest a practical import result for each component: retain its source family and feature modes, identify the renderable representation, and record which consumers can use it. A mesh can be supported for opaque raster and shadows while its animated ray geometry or GI proxy remains unsupported. A volume can be supported through integration while having no triangle representation at all. Compatibility should therefore be reported as family × feature mode × renderer consumer, with an explicit approximation or rejection for missing combinations.

For Arda, the first milestone can reproduce the baseline room and its captured extension: indexed meshes, the planter's instances, the mannequin's skinning, the hinged door's transform hierarchy, and material/light contracts. Then add the wall panel, floor exhibit, effects station, and control display through stable interfaces for mutable geometry, curves/particles, volumes, and scene services. That is an implementation order, not permission to silently discard the later families when loading a scene. The importer should report them with component names and concrete unsupported modes. Once the relevant adapters exist, the same shared scene identity and update machinery can feed depth, surface shading, shadows, ray tracing, and GI without pretending that all five consumers need identical geometry.
